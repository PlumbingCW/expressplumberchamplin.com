Water

Treated well water and the hardness question

Coon Rapids pumps its water from 24 wells and treats it for iron and manganese. The city's materials give hardness as 16 to 18 without stating a unit, so we do not quote it as a firm number. A tap test or a call to city utilities settles it for your address.

Near the river

Coon Rapids Dam Regional Park sits at 9750 Egret Blvd on the Mississippi. Homes near the river and its terraces can see groundwater in wet seasons, and low spots hold water longer. If your basement shows seepage, start with basement water control and a working pump.

Local notes

More about plumbing in Coon Rapids

Coon Rapids is large, and homes range from mid-century neighborhoods to newer developments. The age of the home usually predicts the problem. Older homes show up with slow drains and aging water heaters, and newer homes show up with fixture failures and sump pumps that run hard after storms.

The Metropolitan Council's sewer rehabilitation work in the city reminds owners that sewer pipes age. If your home has had repeated backups, a camera inspection is the quickest way to learn whether your own line needs work.

The city's water is treated for iron and manganese, according to the city, and hardness is described in city materials without a stated unit. That is why we suggest a tap test before any softener purchase. Our softener and filtration page explains how to decide.

If you share a street with neighbors who recently had work done, ask how it went. A good plumber welcomes the question. We tell you what we find and give you the price before work starts.
