Repiping replaces the supply pipes inside a house, and it is usually a planned project. We book same-day appointments when available for the assessment, and our 24/7 line is open when a burst or leak turns planned work into an emergency. See burst pipe repair for that.

When it makes sense

Signs that repiping may be worth considering

Repeated leaks

Several leaks in a short time, especially pinholes, can mean the pipe is wearing out everywhere and not in one spot.

Discolored or metallic water

Rust-colored or off-tasting water from old galvanized or corroded pipe can signal internal corrosion.

Falling pressure

Mineral buildup inside old steel pipe narrows it over time and can drop pressure at every fixture.

Known problem material

Some pipe and fitting types from specific decades have failure records. We identify what you have.

Diagnosis

Repair a section or repipe the house

Many homes do not need a full repipe. If a leak is isolated and the rest of the pipe is sound, a section repair is cheaper and less disruptive. We look at the pipe material, its age, the failure pattern and your water quality before we say anything about replacement.

When leaks are hard to find, leak detection can show whether the problem is one spot or many. A pressure test and a meter check often answer the question before any wall is opened.

What to expect

How a repipe is done

Assess

We inspect the pipe, test pressure and decide on a route.

Plan

You get the scope, the materials and the price before work starts.

Replace

New lines are run, usually with limited access holes in walls and ceilings.

Test and patch

The system is pressure tested, and openings are closed up for finish work.

Drywall repair and painting are separate from plumbing work unless we agree otherwise in advance. We tell you which trades are needed before we start, so the plan has no surprises.

FAQs

Questions About Repiping

How do I know if I need a full repipe?

Repeated leaks in different spots, discolored water and falling pressure are warning signs. An assessment tells you whether repair or replacement fits.

Which pipe material is best?

It depends on your house, water and budget. PEX, copper and CPVC each have a place, and we explain the trade-offs.

Will repiping mean opening up walls?

Usually some access is needed. We plan the route to limit it and tell you what to expect.

Do you handle burst pipes at night?

Yes. The line is open 24/7 for burst pipes and major leaks. Planned repiping work is scheduled around your needs.

Can I live in the house during a repipe?

Often, yes. We plan the work to limit time without water and explain the schedule in advance.
