Plumbing in a city built on sand and river terraces

Champlin sits where the Crow and Mississippi rivers meet, on ground the Minnesota DNR describes as part of the Anoka Sand Plain. Sandy soil drains quickly, but low spots near the river can hold water, which is why sump pumps and basement water questions come up often here.

According to the City of Champlin, its Utilities Department runs the water and sanitary sewer systems, and the city's water comes from six wells. If you are not sure whether a problem sits on your side of the property line or the city's, call us and we will tell you where the line stops being yours.

Before you call

Quick checks that help the plumber

  • Note where the water or backup is and when it started.
  • Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff if water is flowing.
  • Take photos for your records and your insurer.
  • Keep children and pets away from sewage or standing water.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before anyone else.

If you are comparing sources, check the plumber's license through the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry. We are happy to answer questions about drain cleaning, water heater replacement or leak detection before you decide.

What counts as a plumbing emergency?

Water you cannot stop, sewage backing up, no water at all, a failed water heater leaking and a burst pipe all qualify. A slow drip can usually wait for a scheduled visit.

What should I do while I wait for the plumber?

Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main, keep people away from sewage and photograph the damage. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the gas utility.
