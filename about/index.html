Emergencies

What counts as an emergency

Water you cannot stop, sewage backing up, no water at all, a leaking or failed water heater and a burst pipe are emergencies. A slow drip or a slow drain can usually wait for a scheduled visit. If you are unsure, call and describe it. We help you decide how urgent it is.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call us. CenterPoint Energy lists 612-372-5050 and asks you to press 1 for a leak.

Safety

Safety comes before repairs

Our first job on an emergency call is to keep people safe. That means helping you shut off water, avoid electrical hazards near wet areas and stay away from sewage. According to the Minnesota Department of Health, every home needs an operational carbon monoxide alarm within 10 feet of each bedroom, and we point out missing alarms when we see them on a water heater or boiler call.

We also keep notes on what we found. If your insurer asks for photos or a description of the damage, we can tell you what to gather. We never promise coverage, because only your insurer can decide that.
