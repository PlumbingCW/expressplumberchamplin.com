Mississippi Crossings is a redevelopment area of about 160 acres along the Mississippi River, south of the Anoka-Champlin bridge. The City of Champlin describes it as a city-led project to replace aging commercial buildings and apartments. It is not a single-family subdivision, so plumbing here works differently from a neighborhood of houses.

The city lists Applewood Pointe, an 85-unit senior cooperative finished in 2019, and a market-rate rental development by Greco with more than 200 units. It also lists an event center, planned riverfront improvements and the Mill Pond Dam rebuilt in 2016.

Who to call

Renter, owner or building manager, the first call differs

If you rent an apartment here, call your property manager first. They own the building's pipes, and many leases require tenants to report problems to management. Calling an outside plumber on your own can cause trouble with your lease.

Owners of a cooperative unit should check their cooperative's rules, because shared systems are usually the cooperative's responsibility. Building managers and business operators are who our commercial work is for.

General plumbing information

Plumbing in larger buildings

Apartment and mixed-use buildings share risers, drain stacks, water heating and sometimes a single main shutoff. A leak or backup can reach neighbors, so speed and a clear shutoff plan matter more than they do in a house.

Restaurants and event spaces add grease traps, floor drains and heavy fixture use. Those systems need regular attention to avoid backups at the wrong moment.

Business buildings

Shops, restaurants and event space need planned and urgent service. See commercial plumbing.

Leaks

A stain, a damp wall or a high water bill can point to a hidden leak. See leak detection.

Backups

Slow or gurgling drains in a shared building need prompt clearing. See drain cleaning.

Line checks

A camera shows what is in a sewer line before anyone guesses. See sewer camera inspection.

Report it fast

Tell your property manager or building owner as soon as you see water, so shared shutoffs can be closed.

Find your valve

Look for the shutoff under the sink, behind the toilet or near the water heater in your unit.

Protect belongings

Move items off the floor and photograph any damage for your records.

Stay clear of sewage

Keep people and pets away from backed-up drains and wash hands after contact.

Safety

If you smell gas or see sewage

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911, then CenterPoint Energy at 612-372-5050. In a multi-unit building, tell your manager as well. For the public water or sewer main, the City of Champlin lists Utilities at 763-421-0154.
