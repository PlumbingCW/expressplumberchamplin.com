Before you call

Who owns the repair, you or the association?

This is the first question in any association community. Lawn and snow are covered, but the page does not say whether exterior items such as roofing or siding are, and it advises checking.

Plumbing inside the home is normally the owner's job. Ask your association for its governing documents if you are unsure, because they spell out the line between shared and private.

General plumbing information

Plumbing in one-level homes

A single-level layout puts all the fixtures on one floor, often close to a slab or a basement. That makes it easy to spot a wet floor, and it makes toilets and showers easier to reach for repair.

Many owners of one-level homes choose them for easier daily living. Comfort-height toilets, lever faucets and walk-in showers are common requests, and each is a straightforward install.

Fixture swaps

Taller toilets and lever handles are a simple upgrade. See toilet repair and installation.

Faucet repair

A dripping or stiff faucet is usually a worn cartridge or valve. See faucet and sink repair.

Hot water

A tank that leaks or stops heating needs a look. See water heater repair.

Drains

Kitchen and bath drains slow down from buildup. See drain cleaning.

Before hiring any plumbing contractor, you can look up their license on the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry website. That check is worth doing for any repair, whoever you call.
