Before you call

Check whether the association or a builder owns the problem

In a managed community, some exterior and shared items can belong to the association instead of the homeowner. Pool, clubhouse and park plumbing is the association's business, so report those to the association first.

Anything inside your own walls is normally yours, including the water heater, fixtures, supply lines and drains. If you are unsure, your association documents or your closing paperwork will say where the line falls.

General plumbing information

Plumbing in larger custom homes

Bigger houses have more plumbing to go wrong simply because there is more of it. More bathrooms, longer pipe runs and a larger water heater or two mean more places for a slow leak to hide. A fixture that sees little use, such as a guest bath, can drip for a long time before anyone notices.

None of that is a defect. It is a reason to look at the water meter now and then, and to keep an eye on spots under sinks and around the base of the water heater.

Water heaters

Larger homes often run bigger tanks or more than one unit. See water heater repair when you have no hot water or a leak at the tank.

Hidden leaks

A meter that moves when every fixture is off points to a leak somewhere. See leak detection.

Planned work

Remodels, a new bar sink or a basement bath start with rough-in. See plumbing installation.

Groundwater

A sump pit, if your home has one, needs a working pump. See sump pump repair.

FAQs

Plumbing Questions in Reserve at Elm Creek

Does the association handle plumbing repairs inside my home?

Usually the association handles shared amenities and the homeowner handles what is inside the house. Your association documents decide the details, so check them if you are unsure.

Who built the homes in Reserve at Elm Creek?

A local real estate team lists Jonathan Homes as developer and Dingman Custom Homes, Lewis Custom Homes and Hanson Builders as builders. Confirm anything about warranties with the builder or your closing paperwork.

How do I tell if I have a hidden leak?

Turn off every fixture, then watch the water meter. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere. Call and we can talk through where to look.

Can you work on a bigger or second water heater?

Yes. Describe the model and the symptom on the phone and we will explain the likely options.
