How to Find a Plumbing Leak in Your Champlin Home

Most hidden leaks can be narrowed down with a water meter, a few checks and some patience. Here is the order that saves time.

Written by the Champlin Express Plumbing team · Published 2026 · 8 min read

A leak does not have to flood a floor to cost you. A toilet that runs on quietly or a supply line weeping inside a wall can waste water for months and show up only in a high bill. The steps below help you find where the water is going before you open any walls.

Step one

Check the water meter

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Look at your water meter and note the reading or watch the small leak indicator. If it moves with everything off, water is escaping somewhere.

Wait an hour without using water and check again. If the reading has changed, you have a leak. If not, the leak may be intermittent or the fixture-level kind.

Step two

Test the toilets

Toilets are the most common hidden leak. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait about 15 minutes without flushing and look in the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks. A worn flapper is a quick repair, and our toilet repair page covers it.

Step three

Look at visible plumbing

  • Check under every sink for wet cabinet floors or drips.
  • Look at the base of the water heater for rust or moisture.
  • Inspect washing machine hoses for bulges and wet spots.
  • Look at exposed pipes in the basement for drips or white mineral crust.
  • Check outdoor spigots and hose connections.
Step four

Look for signals in the house

Stained ceilings or walls

A brown ring that grows points at a leak above.

Peeling paint or bubbling drywall

Moisture inside a wall pushes the finish outward.

Musty smells

Hidden damp air has a smell even when nothing is visible.

Warm spots on the floor

A leaking hot line under a slab can warm the floor above.

Sounds of running water

Hissing or trickling when nothing is on suggests a pressurized leak.

Step five

Decide if the leak is inside or outside

If the meter moves but nothing inside the house shows a leak, the problem may be in the line from the meter to the house. A soft spot in the yard, a patch of greener grass or a drop in pressure supports that. See water line repair for what happens next.

Close the main shutoff in the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

Pressure

Is the pressure normal

High pressure strains pipes and fixtures. A gauge that screws onto a hose spigot shows pressure. Many homes target a range recommended by the fixture maker, and a regulator that fails can raise it. If the gauge reads high, a plumber can replace the regulator.

When to call

When to stop searching and call a plumber

Call when the meter moves but you cannot find the cause, when water appears on a ceiling, when a slab feels warm or when the leak is behind a wall. A plumber's equipment can listen to pipes, trace lines and measure moisture without cutting anything open. Our leak detection page explains the methods.

Sound and heat

Using sound, touch and temperature

Quiet houses make leaks audible. At night, with everything off, walk through the house and listen near walls, the water heater and the meter. A faint hiss or trickle with no fixture running is a clue. Pressurized leaks hiss, while drain leaks gurgle or drip.

Feel the floor in areas you suspect. A warm patch on a slab can mark a leaking hot water line beneath it. A cool, damp patch near an outside wall may point to a cold line or a foundation leak. Mark the spot with tape and tell the plumber.

What to do

What to do once you find water

Shut off the water to the fixture or the whole house, then mop up and ventilate. Photograph everything, including the meter reading, before you clean. If the water has soaked drywall or insulation, remove wet material quickly, since mold can start after a day or two.

Write down the date you noticed the leak and any recent high bills. Insurers and utilities often ask for this. If the leak is behind a wall or under a slab, resist the urge to cut it open yourself. Locating it first limits the damage and the repair.

FAQs

Leak Questions in Champlin

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

Turn off all water and watch the meter. If it moves, water is escaping.

Who do I call about the line at the street?

Will a running toilet raise my bill?

Yes. It wastes water quietly.

Can a leak be under my slab?

Yes. A warm spot or a running meter without visible signs can point to it.
