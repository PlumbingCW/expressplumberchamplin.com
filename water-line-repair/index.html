Urgent situations

Signs a water line has failed

A wet or soft patch in the yard

Water surfacing between the street and the house, especially in dry weather, points to a break in the line.

No water, or very little

A severed or collapsed line can cut water off completely.

Pressure that dropped for no reason

A slow leak can drop pressure across the house before anything shows on the surface.

A meter that runs with everything off

Water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. See leak detection for how to confirm it.

Low pressure

Low water pressure: line, regulator or fixture

Low pressure is not always a bad line. If one fixture is weak, look at the aerator or cartridge. If the whole house is weak, check the main shutoff and any pressure regulator. If those are sound and pressure fell suddenly, a service line leak is a real possibility.

Questions about pressure on the city side belong with the Utilities Department, which maintains the water distribution system.

Champlin facts

Materials, depth and utility locates

The City of Champlin's 2026 water quality report states that its service line inventory contains 0 lead, 0 galvanized lines requiring replacement, 0 unknown material and 8,094 non-lead service lines as of October 20, 2025. That is a statement about the city's inventory and it does not describe the plumbing inside your house.

Minnesota's plumbing code (Minn. R. 4714.0609) puts building supply and yard piping at least 12 inches below the maximum local frost depth. Before digging, call Gopher State One Call at 811, and ask the Champlin building inspections division about permit and inspection requirements.

Related causes

Underground leaks and sandy soil

The Minnesota DNR describes the Anoka Sand Plain as level to gently rolling with sandy river terraces. Water from a buried leak can soak away in sandy soil, so a dry yard does not prove the line is sound. A meter test and listening equipment can find a leak that never reaches the surface.

Standards

What the plumbing code says about depth

According to Minnesota's plumbing code (Minn. R. 4714.0609), supply piping outside the building is installed at least 12 inches below the maximum local frost depth. A replacement line is planned with that rule in mind.

FAQs

Questions About Water Line Repair & Replacement

Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Champlin?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. We book same-day appointments when available and tell you the price before work starts.

Who do I call if the break is at the street?

Call us, and also the City of Champlin Utilities Department, which lists water service maintenance and location at 763-421-0154.

Can a water line be replaced without digging up the whole yard?

Sometimes. Trenchless methods use a smaller number of access points, depending on the path and condition.

Why did my water pressure drop suddenly?

Check the main shutoff first, then any regulator. If both are fine, a service line leak is possible, and a meter test helps confirm it.

How deep should a water line be?

Minnesota's plumbing code requires supply piping at least 12 inches below the maximum local frost depth.
