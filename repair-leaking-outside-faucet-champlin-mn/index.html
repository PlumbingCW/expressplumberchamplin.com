How to Repair a Leaking Outside Faucet in Champlin Before the Freeze

An outdoor faucet that drips in summer can burst in winter. Here is how to fix it, and how to keep it from freezing.

Written by the Champlin Express Plumbing team · Published 2026 · 7 min read

An outdoor faucet is the part of a plumbing system most exposed to Minnesota winters. A drip in July is a nuisance. The same fixture with a hose attached in January can burst the pipe inside the wall, and the water may not show until spring.

This guide explains how to repair the common leaks and how to protect the faucet before it freezes.

Where it leaks

Find the source of the leak

Simple repairs

Replace a worn washer or packing

Turn off the water

Use the shutoff inside the house for that line, then open the faucet to drain it.

Remove the handle

Take off the handle screw and the packing nut.

Replace the washer or packing

Pull the stem out and replace the worn washer or packing.

Reassemble and test

Turn the water back on slowly and check for drips.

Brass stems wear over time, and a new stem is often cheaper than repeated washer swaps. If the faucet body itself is corroded, replacement is the better value. See faucet repair for how we decide.

Frost-free spigots

Why frost-free faucets still freeze

A frost-free spigot has a long stem so the shutoff seat sits inside the warm part of the wall. That design only works if water drains out of the barrel after you close it. A hose left attached traps water in the barrel, and the freeze can crack the pipe.

Frost-free spigots must also slope slightly downward toward the outside so they drain. If water drips out for a minute after closing, it is working. If the faucet stays wet inside, check the slope.

Before winter

Protect the faucet

  • Disconnect every hose in the fall, including splitters and timers.
  • Close the interior shutoff for the line, if there is one, and open the outside faucet to drain.
  • Install an insulated faucet cover on older spigots.
  • Keep the interior line from freezing by sealing gaps where the pipe enters the wall.
  • Replace an old nonfrost faucet with a frost-free model.

According to the American Red Cross, extra care is wise on freezing nights. Opening cabinets to let warm air reach pipes on exterior walls and letting a trickle run from a faucet can help prevent frozen pipes.

If it already froze

What to do if you suspect a burst

If the outside faucet froze and you see water dripping inside the basement when the temperature rises, shut off the water to that line right away. Do not wait. A burst pipe can release a lot of water quickly once the ice melts. Our burst pipe repair page explains how we handle those calls, and the line is open 24/7.

Champlin note

Champlin note

Frost depth varies across Minnesota. The state's plumbing code sets rules for how deep supply piping must be buried relative to local frost depth, and it does not use a single statewide number. Piping that runs outside should be checked against the frost depth that applies to your area.

What to do

What to do if a hose bib burst inside the wall

A burst behind a spigot often shows itself as water running down an interior wall or into the basement when the weather warms. Close the main shutoff right away, since the interior shutoff for that line may be hard to reach. Then open a faucet low in the house to drain the pipes and relieve pressure.

Photograph the damage and move belongings away from the wet area. Call the line so a plumber can cut out the damaged section and rebuild the connection. Drywall and insulation will need drying, and that is a separate job to plan.

Choosing a replacement

Picking a better outdoor faucet

If the old faucet is corroded or has frozen before, a frost-free model with a vacuum breaker is a sound replacement. The vacuum breaker keeps water from flowing backward from a hose into the house supply, which matters if the hose sits in a bucket or a pool. Pick a length that puts the shutoff seat inside the heated part of the wall.

Match the faucet to the wall thickness, and install it with a slight downward slope toward the outside so it drains. Our faucet repair page covers repair and replacement.

FAQs

Outdoor Faucet Questions in Champlin

Why does my outside faucet drip when it is off?

A worn washer or valve seat is the usual cause.

Do I need to shut off the water inside before winter?

If there is an interior shutoff for the line, yes. Then drain the outside faucet.

How do I know a frost-free faucet drains?

Water should drip out for a few seconds after you close it.

What if water appears inside after a freeze?

Shut off the main and call. The line may have burst.

Is there one frost depth for Minnesota?

No. The state code uses zones, and local depth governs.
