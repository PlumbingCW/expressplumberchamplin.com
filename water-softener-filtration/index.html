Do you need one

Start with your water, not a sales pitch

Champlin's drinking water comes from six city wells, and the city's latest water quality report states disinfectant and fluoride levels but does not give a hardness number. That means the honest first step is to test your own tap, or ask the City of Champlin Utilities Department about hardness.

The U.S. Geological Survey classifies water as soft up to 60 milligrams per liter, moderately hard from 61 to 120, hard from 121 to 180 and very hard above 180. The Minnesota Department of Health says that if hardness is above 7 grains per gallon, or 120 milligrams per liter, you might need a softener.

Softener types

Demand-based or timer-based

The Minnesota Pollution Control Agency advises replacing timer-based softeners with demand-based ones. A timer unit regenerates on a schedule whether it needs to or not. A demand unit regenerates when the resin is used up, which saves salt and water.

The salt and chloride issue

What softener brine does to Minnesota water

Softener brine drains to the wastewater system. The Minnesota Pollution Control Agency says wastewater treatment plants cannot remove chloride, and brine from softeners is a source of it. Setting your softener to use less salt is better for local lakes and rivers, and it also saves money.

The Minnesota Department of Health also notes that softening adds sodium to the water, and that water used to regenerate the resin ends up as waste. People on a sodium-restricted diet should ask their doctor.

Softener repair

Softener repair and replacement

Hard water returns

A softener that stops softening may have a depleted or fouled resin bed, a failed valve or a control problem.

Salt bridge or mush

A hardened crust or sludge in the brine tank blocks the cycle. Clearing it is a service call.

Constant running or noise

A valve that does not seat or a control board fault can cause continuous cycling.

Reduced pressure

A clogged resin bed or fouled valve can restrict flow.

If the unit is old, repair costs and age decide whether to replace it. Some callers ask about swapping a softener for an on-demand water heater at the same time. That is a bigger project, and our tankless water heater page explains why hardness matters for it.

Filtration

Filtration for taste, odor and wells

City water in Champlin is disinfected with chlorine and has fluoride added, according to the city's report. A carbon filter or a reverse osmosis system under a kitchen sink reduces taste and odor from chlorine. Whole-house filters are a larger project, and the right one depends on what is in your water.

If your home is on a private well, as most homes in Dayton outside the municipal service area are, treatment starts with a lab test of your own water. A well can have iron, manganese or other minerals that the city treats at its plants and that you would need to treat yourself.

What to do

What to do before you buy

Test your tap first and write down the result. Ask any seller to explain what the equipment removes and what it adds, including sodium and chloride. Avoid signing anything on the spot, and compare at least two quotes in writing.

If you have a health condition that limits sodium, ask your doctor before choosing softened water for drinking. A plumber can install a separate unsoftened tap at the kitchen sink if that is useful.

FAQs

Questions About Water Softener & Filtration

What is the hardness of Champlin water?

The city's latest water quality report does not give a hardness value. Test your tap or ask the City of Champlin Utilities Department.

Do I need a softener?

The Minnesota Department of Health says you might need one if hardness is above 7 grains per gallon or 120 milligrams per liter. Test first.

Are softeners bad for the environment?

Softener brine adds chloride to wastewater, and treatment plants cannot remove it. Demand-based units and lower salt settings reduce the load.

Can you repair a softener you did not install?

Often, yes. Tell us the brand and symptom when you call.

Do I need a filter if I have city water?

Not always. Filters help with taste and odor. Your own preference and a water test decide.
